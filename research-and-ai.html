Research, AI, and Plans for 2027

I am currently applying for postdoc positions.

I had originally planned to publish some works around this time, and dwell on other research problems long into the future. Due to the accelerated progress of AI, I have decided in recent months to publish almost all of my current projects. The exception is a joint work with the legendary Tom Moshaiov, Liza Popova, and Ran Tessler, in which AI is used only for computer simulations, digesting material, auditing, and writing—not for idea generation or problem solving.

This accelerated pace is not the working pace I prefer, nor one I suggest to others or plan to keep. It reflects my choice to publish works into which I have put substantial effort over recent years, and which may soon be “one prompt away,” as I once heard. It also reflects similar feelings of panic among some of my coauthors, and my desire to make them happy.

This is far from an optimal situation. Honestly, I really hate it. I would not want this accelerated pace to send the wrong message: I do not think that hyper-publication is the right course of action these days. I hope that me writing these may reduce your anxiety (which I share) of the flood of publications.

I would be very happy to discuss new projects for 2027. These could also mean studying something new together (I never really understood real analysis… 😳), writing a book, and, of course, doing mathematical research.

An important note: I am not anti-AI in any way, and I do not label myself an “artisanal mathematician,” as some others have. Actually, I am quite the opposite: I believe that having new tools to help us understand phenomena, digest ideas, write papers, and run simulations is wonderful and has great potential for mathematics. I even support using AI for idea generation in some cases.

Nevertheless, I do not feel that current AI systems quite fit this role. I feel they are trained to function as mathematical genies, and not to be helpful tools. Specifically, interacting with them is currently not conversational, intuitive, or geometric.
